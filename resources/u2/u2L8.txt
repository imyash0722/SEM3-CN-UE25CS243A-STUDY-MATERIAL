SLIDE 86: Go-Back-N (GBN): Sender Perspective
================================================================================

COMPUTER NETWORKS: Go-Back-N: Sender

Sender Window Rules:
• Sliding window of up to $N$ consecutive transmitted but unacknowledged packets.
• Range of sequence numbers: $k$-bit field yields sequence numbers $0$ to $2^k - 1$.

Sequence Number Space:
+-------------------+-----------------------+-------------------+-------------------+
| Already ACKed     | Sent, UnACKed         | Usable, Not Yet   | Not Usable Until  |
|                   | ("In-Flight", up to N)| Sent (within win) | Window Slides     |
+-------------------+-----------------------+-------------------+-------------------+
 ^                   ^                       ^                   ^
 0                 base                  nextseqnum            base+N

Key Sender Mechanisms:
• Cumulative ACK: `ACK(n)` acknowledges all packets up to and including sequence number $n$.
• Window Slide: Upon receiving `ACK(n)`, the window base advances forward to $n + 1$.
• Single Timer: The sender maintains a single timer for the oldest in-flight (unACKed) packet (`base`).
• Timeout Event: Upon timeout, the sender retransmits ALL $N$ packets currently in the window starting from `base` ("Goes back $N$ steps").

================================================================================
SLIDE 87: Go-Back-N (GBN): Receiver Perspective
================================================================================

COMPUTER NETWORKS: Go-Back-N: Receiver

Receiver Behavior:
• Strictly In-Order Delivery:
  - Expects packet with sequence number `rcv_base`.
  - Upon receiving in-order packet: Delivers to application, sends `ACK(rcv_base)`, advances `rcv_base = rcv_base + 1`.
• Handling Out-of-Order Packets:
  - If a packet arrives with a higher or out-of-order sequence number:
    * The receiver DISCARDS it (does NOT buffer it!).
    * Re-sends an ACK for the highest in-order packet successfully received so far.
• State Memory:
  - The GBN receiver needs to maintain only a single variable: `rcv_base`.

SLIDE 88: Go-Back-N in Action: Timeline Example
================================================================================

COMPUTER NETWORKS: Go-Back-N in action

Timeline ($N = 4$):
1. Sender sends `pkt0`, `pkt1`, `pkt2`, `pkt3`. Window is full [0, 1, 2, 3].
2. Receiver receives `pkt0`, sends `ack0`.
3. Receiver receives `pkt1`, sends `ack1`.
4. `pkt2` is LOST in transit!
5. Receiver receives `pkt3` (out of order!). Receiver DISCARDS `pkt3` and re-sends `ack1`.
6. Sender receives `ack0` -> Window slides to [1, 2, 3, 4], sends `pkt4`.
7. Sender receives `ack1` -> Window slides to [2, 3, 4, 5], sends `pkt5`.
8. Receiver receives `pkt4` and `pkt5` -> DISCARDS both and re-sends `ack1`.
9. Timeout for `pkt2` fires at sender!
10. Sender retransmits ALL in-flight packets in current window: `pkt2`, `pkt3`, `pkt4`, `pkt5`!

SLIDE 89: Go-Back-N Numerical Problem: Problem Statement
================================================================================

COMPUTER NETWORKS: Go-Back-N: example

Problem Statement:
In a Go-Back-N (GBN) protocol:
• Window size $N = 10$.
• Exactly 10 distinct packets (numbered 1 to 10) must be transmitted.
• Network Failure Rule: Every 5th packet transmitted across the network is lost (i.e., transmission #5, #10, #15, #20... are lost).

Question:
How many total packet transmissions are required to successfully deliver all 10 packets?

================================================================================
SLIDE 90: Go-Back-N Numerical Problem: Answer
================================================================================

COMPUTER NETWORKS: Go-Back-N: example

Answer: 18 Transmissions

================================================================================
SLIDE 91: Go-Back-N Numerical Problem: Step-by-Step Breakdown
================================================================================

COMPUTER NETWORKS: Go-Back-N: example (Detailed Solution)

Round 1 — Initial Transmission of Packets 1 to 10 (Transmissions 1–10):
• Transmissions 1, 2, 3, 4: Packets 1, 2, 3, 4 delivered successfully.
• Transmission 5: Packet 5 is LOST (5th transmission!).
• Transmissions 6, 7, 8, 9: Packets 6, 7, 8, 9 arrive out of order; receiver DISCARDS all of them!
• Transmission 10: Packet 10 is LOST (10th transmission!).
Receiver has successfully delivered packets 1–4. Sender times out on packet 5 and goes back to packet 5.

Round 2 — Retransmit Packets 5 to 10 (Transmissions 11–16):
• Transmissions 11, 12, 13, 14: Packets 5, 6, 7, 8 delivered successfully.
• Transmission 15: Packet 9 is LOST (15th transmission!).
• Transmission 16: Packet 10 arrives out of order; receiver DISCARDS it.
Receiver now has successfully delivered packets 1–8. Sender times out on packet 9 and goes back to packet 9.

Round 3 — Retransmit Packets 9 and 10 (Transmissions 17–18):
• Transmission 17: Packet 9 delivered successfully (17 is not a multiple of 5).
• Transmission 18: Packet 10 delivered successfully (18 is not a multiple of 5).

Total Transmissions Required = 10 + 6 + 2 = 18 Transmissions!
